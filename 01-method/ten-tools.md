# The Ten Tools: an in-house lawyer's entry kit into Ω

The Ten Tools come from *Ten Tools for In-House Lawyers*, a field guide the author wrote and used to train lawyers moving into corporate practice. They are read here as the kit an incoming General Counsel picks up on the first day, when there is work but no designed capability: the **SP0 Blank Canvas** entry in `starting-points.md`. The starting-point plan sequences Ω across a first 90 days; the tools are how the lawyer gathers the raw material each element consumes, by asking, looking and listening rather than by importing a peer's program. Each tool below states what it is, the Ω elements and prompts it feeds, and the starting point it serves. Every element and prompt identifier is one defined in `elements.md` and `03-prompts/`; nothing new is introduced here.

## Tool 1: Who, what, when, where, why and how

Interrogate the business before advising it. Ask who does what, when and where, why and how, over and over, at every meeting. The why matters most: an activity that cannot be tied to how the business creates and captures value is one to question at the root. This is the LEARN work of the incoming General Counsel at SP0. It feeds **L1 External Context**, **L2 Internal Context** and **L4 Stakeholders**, and the why routes straight to **A2 Objectives**, so that identified items later attach to an objective rather than float free. Prompts: `L1`, `L2`, `L4`, then `A2`.

## Tool 2: Risk categorization, systems, communications and capacities

Sort risk with the known-knowns grid, then route each quadrant to a response. The grid is the lawyer's first pass at **A3 Identification** and **A4 Assessment**; the responses are where Ω carries the load.

| Quadrant | Handbook response | Ω element |
|---|---|---|
| Known knowns | Handle one by one through Systems | A5 Design, P1 Controls, P2 Policies |
| Unknown knowns | Surface from the silos through Communications | P3 Communication, P6 Notification |
| Known unknowns | Training for the legal team, Education for the Client | P4 Education |
| Unknown unknowns | No system; hold slack capacity, let "good enough is good enough" govern the rest | R1 Monitoring, against the appetite set in A1 Direction |

The last quadrant is the one the method cannot design away: capacity kept in reserve, watched by **R1 Monitoring** and bounded by the appetite stated in **A1 Direction**. Serves SP0 first, and SP1 Topical when one risk is already burning. Prompts: `A3`, `A4`, then `A5`, `P3`, `P4`.

## Tool 3: Speed-learning

Build an evolving, deliberately partial model of an unfamiliar subject through fast iterative search, the right jargon and a beginner's mind, then stop short of what the model does not yet support. This is the engine behind **L1 External Context**: scan on cadence and on trigger, extract candidate authority sources, and know how much the reading will bear. It feeds **L2 Internal Context** and **A3 Identification**. Serves SP0 and SP1 Topical, where a subject must be learned against a deadline. Prompts: `L1`, `L2`.

## Tool 4: Mapping people, real and unreal

Model two kinds of person: the real people who run the organization and the legal entities that do not. For the real people, get the organization chart and watch where work bends around key individuals rather than systems; this is **L2 Internal Context** and **L4 Stakeholders**. For the entities, draw the structure from first accounting principles. The handbook's Ten Step Method for mapping corporate entities, the author's own method, reads in three passes:

| Pass | Steps | What you draw |
|---|---|---|
| The statements | 1 to 2 | A simple balance sheet for each entity at Time 0 and Time 1, with an income statement between them, broken out by primary business line |
| Who controls | 3 to 6 | Each class of equity (common, preferred, options); how the board is selected on those voting powers; the management the board appoints, at the top of the org chart; each class of debt, tied to the assets that secure it |
| How they connect | 7 to 10 | Relationships between entities (shareholders, management, assets, liabilities, contracts), then workflows and operations, then income and cash flows, then internal and external contracts, each mapped onto the entities |

The map surfaces conflicts of interest and governance problems quickly, and it is the backbone of Tool 9. It feeds **L2 Internal Context**, **L4 Stakeholders** and **A3 Identification**. Serves SP0 and SP2 Discipline. Prompts: `L2`, `L4`.

## Tool 5: Business mapping

Model the whole business on the Business Model Canvas of Osterwalder and Pigneur (*Business Model Generation*), reading its nine blocks with the internal Client: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key activities, key resources, key partnerships and cost structure. Then ask the three closing questions the handbook ends on: does it make sense, could it be better, and how can legal help. The canvas is a structured **L2 Internal Context** read whose value propositions feed **A2 Objectives** and whose key activities and partnerships feed **A3 Identification**. Serves SP0. Prompts: `L2`, `A2`.

## Tool 6: Legal service systems

Run the legal function as five Systems rather than ad hoc work: contracts, advisory and projects, litigation support, training and education. Build each in three stages, how work comes in, how it is processed, how it goes out, and record the output so it can be re-used and so improvement is, in the handbook's phrase, "forced to occur." This tool is Ω applied to the legal team itself: **A5 Design** designs the capability, **P1 Controls** and **P2 Policies** operate it, **R1 Monitoring** watches it and **R3 Improvement** closes the loop the recorded learning opens. Serves SP0, when the function is built from nothing, and SP3 Element, when one system is broken. Prompts: `A5`, `P1`, `P2`, `R1`, `R3`.

## Tool 7: Contracts

Re-orient contracts away from the classroom and read each one as five views at once (Ten Tools, pp. 8 to 9): a business plan, an agreement about the future, an agreement about risks, a puzzle under limited and sometimes false information, and an integration of at least two parties' risk matrices with their business needs. The five views are the lawyer's framing for **A4 Assessment**, where the two matrices meet, and they operate through **P2 Policies** (contract standards and the must-have for each deal) and **P1 Controls** (early review, the approval gate). Obligations extracted from contracts route to **A3 Identification**. Serves SP1 and SP3. Prompts: `P2`, `P1`, `A4`.

## Tool 8: Training and education

Build competence on both sides of the desk: training for the legal team, education for the internal Clients who are the first to meet a legal issue and raise a request. The better the Client spots issues, the better the outcomes. This is **P4 Education**, with its role-to-obligation curriculum, carried on the channels of **P3 Communication**. Serves SP0 and SP3. Prompts: `P4`, `P3`.

## Tool 9: Advisory (M&A)

Treat due diligence, valuation and integration as one activity, run together by the same people or in close communication, started early (Ten Tools, p. 31), so that the information the lawyer earns in diligence is used in the price and in the plan rather than filed and forgotten. Diligence is an **L1 External Context** and **L2 Internal Context** read, built on the entity map of Tool 4; its findings populate **A3 Identification** and **A4 Assessment**, and the inquiry discipline is **P7 Inquiry**. Serves SP2 Discipline and SP1 Topical. Prompts: `A3`, `A4`, `P7`.

## Tool 10: Litigation support and management

Do not run litigation in-house. Outsource it and manage it actively on the Client's behalf (Ten Tools, p. 32), keeping management informed that litigation is one option among many, and address the cause and context so the next dispute is avoided or better met. The intake, investigation and response map to **P6 Notification**, **P7 Inquiry** and **P8 Response**, and the cause-and-context work feeds **R3 Improvement**. This is the **SP4 Crisis** entry, where response is already running when the mandate arrives. Prompts: `P7`, `P8`.

## How the kit sits in Ω

The Ten Tools are LEARN-and-build instruments, not a rival cycle. Tools 1 and 3 do the LEARN reading; Tools 4 and 5 model the organization for it; Tool 2 opens ALIGN; Tool 6 designs and runs the capability across PERFORM and REVIEW; Tools 7 to 10 are the four Systems in operation. Worked in order at SP0, they carry an incoming General Counsel through the first-90-days sequence in `starting-points.md`, and they point at the same Universal Outcomes the rest of the method serves, above all **U5 integrate and improve decision-making** and **U6 prevent, detect and correct**. For a whole-capability snapshot once the kit has been used, run `../03-prompts/M0-maturity-self-assessment.md`.
